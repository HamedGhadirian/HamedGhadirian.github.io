Hi, I'm Hamed, a 3D generalist from Tehran. I studied Computer Science, then fell for art, and now I'm doing an MA in Illustration. So I live somewhere between the two.

Most of my 3D work is stylized props and environments. I often sketch an idea before I model it, and sometimes I go the other way: for some of my paintings, I set up the scene in Blender and paint over it. I've sold two asset packs on the Unreal Marketplace: Middle Eastern weapons with Persian patterns, because I wanted my own culture in my work, and a stylized environment inspired by Dota 2. The piece I still love most is a cassette player I designed myself, from a sketch to a short animation.

I like designing tools, small systems and automations that make unpleasant work easier. It starts with a slow or annoying task in my own day, but what really drives me is when the problem isn't only mine, and my solution helps other people too. Testing and paying attention to details are a big part of how I work: I use the thing, find where it breaks or feels wrong, and fix it until it works. I do something similar beyond tools: before a meeting or a new project, I usually do the research, think the problems through and write a plan. It's invisible work, but it makes things go smoothly for everyone.

On a team, I watch where people's time goes, and when the same slow task keeps coming back, I build an editor tool for it. At home, I've designed a Blender add-on, an on-screen dictionary ([TLDR Vocab](https://github.com/HamedPhixer/TLDR-Vocab)) and a habit tracker ([Daybook](https://github.com/HamedPhixer/Semi-Automatic-Daybook)). So far, AI has written the code for my tools, but I don't like not having full control. I want to understand what's going on underneath, so now I'm learning to code: right now, Unity and C#. I also like knowing how things work. To understand 3D better, I read Real-Time Rendering, not word by word like an engineer, but as an artist who wanted to see what happens behind the scenes. I tested its ideas in Unreal ([Real-Time Rendering Lab](https://github.com/HamedPhixer/Real-TimeRenderingUE5Lab)), and halfway through I saw how much the node graphs were hiding from me. That pushed me toward code too.

I'd rather know a little about many parts of game making, and go deep in a few. I started in 3D modelling, and I'm growing toward tech art and code. Outside work, I draw, read books, play games, spend time with family and friends, and keep my Duolingo streak alive.
